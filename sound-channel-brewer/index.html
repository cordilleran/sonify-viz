Sound Channel Brewer

Pick two auditory channels for two variables and hear them together on random data, with a few levels each, before you bind a real data lane to them. Each cell of the matrix is a pair; click it or press Enter to hear it.

For hearing, not for ranking. A pair that sounds clear here is not proven to work on your data. The page states no listening-test results. Headphones help with pan and fine loudness steps. Sound starts only when you press Play or pick a pair.

How it works

Temporal pattern
The share of events whose level follows a slow curve over the loop instead of chance, so you can test whether a pattern comes through.
V3
A third variable over time. It does not sit in the matrix: it changes how many events sound (density) or how fast they come (pace), not how each note sounds.
Drum and drone
The drum lane is a first look at a percussion layer: one drum on sixteen steps under the pair, with one variable, V4, on one of its channels at four levels. The drone bed is a held chord on the key's root and carries no data.
Seed
The same seed always gives the same loop, but next-door seeds share nothing, so the dial browses, it does not glide. Variation is the glide: it moves this seed's numbers toward a partner seed's, keeping their spread, so 10 to 30 % gives a near cousin of what you found. Cousins picks a different partner.
Transition
How long a change takes to fade in, from a cut to twelve seconds.
Trail
Every sound you play for two seconds or more is added to the trail, newest first. Click one to hear it again; star it to keep it. The trail and stars stay in this browser on this device only, so copy stars as JSON to keep them elsewhere. The page address follows the sound, so Back steps through your trail and a bookmark keeps one.
How the data are made

Design logic from Channel Brewer by Enrico Bertini (a visual-channel picker, itself after ColorBrewer), taken as an idea only; this page is new code. Synthesis runs in the page: eight channels in the matrix with more on the bench, eleven synthetic voices, a drum lane with five drums and a drone bed, a choice of key and scale (D major pentatonic to start), a 4-second loop, peak capped at −6 dBFS with a limiter after it. Random data from a fixed seed. Formulas are set with KaTeX (MIT), shipped with the page.

Keys: arrows move in the matrix, Enter plays the pair, Space stops (in the matrix), Escape stops anywhere.
