Sound Channel Brewer

Pick two auditory channels for two variables and hear them together on random data, with a few levels each, before you bind a real data lane to them. Each cell of the matrix is a pair; click it or press Enter to hear it.

For hearing, not for ranking. A pair that sounds clear here is not proven to work on your data. The page states no listening-test results. Headphones help with pan and fine loudness steps. Sound starts only when you press Play or pick a pair.

The drum lane is a first look at a percussion layer: one drum on sixteen steps under the pair, with one variable, V4, on one of its channels at four levels.

V3 is a third variable over time. It does not sit in the matrix: it changes how many events sound (density) or how fast they come (pace), not how each note sounds.

How the data are made

Temporal pattern: the share of events whose level follows a slow curve over the loop instead of chance, so you can test whether a pattern comes through.

Play a pair from JSON

Paste a pair copied from this page, or open a saved .json file, then press Play. The JSON carries the seed, so the same JSON gives the same loop.

Design logic from Channel Brewer by Enrico Bertini (a visual-channel picker, itself after ColorBrewer), taken as an idea only; this page is new code. Synthesis runs in the page: eight channels in the matrix with more on the bench, eleven synthetic voices and a drum lane with five drums, D major pentatonic, a 4-second loop, peak capped at −6 dBFS with a limiter after it. Random data from a fixed seed. Formulas are set with KaTeX (MIT), shipped with the page.

Keys: arrows move in the matrix, Enter plays the pair, Space stops (in the matrix), Escape stops anywhere.
